# Philosophies: Design, Product, Research, Quality, AI, Taste

## Design Philosophy
Nishant believes design is about creating value, not producing interfaces. Great design begins with understanding the problem, challenging assumptions, and balancing customer needs, business goals, and technical constraints before moving into execution. Visual craft is important, but it is the outcome of good thinking, judgment, and taste rather than the objective itself.

## Product Philosophy
Nishant believes great products emerge from the intersection of customer needs, business goals, and technical feasibility. Product teams should avoid treating requirements as fixed answers and instead continuously validate assumptions, question trade-offs, and iterate based on evidence.

## Research Philosophy
Research should reduce uncertainty, not validate predetermined ideas. Every designer should regularly speak with users, while dedicated researchers scale strategic initiatives.

## Quality Philosophy
Nishant believes quality is not achieved through last-minute reviews but through clear standards, ownership, and continuous feedback. Design systems, critiques, QA, and governance exist to make quality repeatable rather than dependent on individual effort.

## Experience Philosophy
Nishant believes users don't experience features in isolation — they experience journeys. Individual interactions should therefore be designed as part of a coherent end-to-end experience rather than optimized independently. His work consistently focuses on reducing friction, building confidence, and creating continuity across the customer journey instead of maximizing individual screens or isolated metrics.

## AI Philosophy
Nishant sees AI as an amplifier of human capability rather than a replacement for it. He encourages designers to automate repetitive work such as research synthesis, documentation, QA, and knowledge retrieval, creating more time for problem framing, collaboration, and decision-making.

While AI can dramatically amplify speed and productivity, it cannot replace human judgment, taste, or curation. AI can generate many possibilities, but deciding which one is worth building — and why — remains the designer's responsibility. His philosophy is simple: AI should automate effort, not judgment.

## Taste
Nishant believes taste is the ability to consistently recognize what creates the greatest value. AI can generate options, but judgment and taste determine which ideas are worth pursuing, refining, or discarding.
