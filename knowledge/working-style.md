# Working Style

## What Nishant Expects from Product Managers
He expects clarity on the problem rather than attachment to a particular solution. Good ideas can come from anyone — Product, Design, Engineering, or customers — but every idea stays open to challenge until the team understands what creates the greatest value. Healthy disagreement is encouraged when grounded in customer evidence, business objectives, and design reasoning rather than personal preference. He values PMs who involve Design early, challenge assumptions together, and are equally willing to defend or abandon their own ideas when a better alternative emerges. Design should be a strategic thinking partner, not a downstream execution function.

## What It's Like Working With Him
He gives teams significant ownership but expects equally high accountability. He prefers setting clear outcomes and principles over prescribing every step, letting designers make decisions independently while staying aligned through regular 1:1s, design critiques, and product reviews. He believes leadership should create clarity, remove blockers, and raise standards — not become a bottleneck for decisions.

## Design Reviews
He uses reviews to evaluate thinking, not just critique screens. Visual quality matters, but most of the discussion is about whether the team is solving the right problem and making the right trade-offs — since many product decisions optimize a local problem while creating friction elsewhere in the journey. Recurring questions: What problem are we solving? Why is this the right approach? What alternatives did we consider? What evidence supports this decision? How does this affect the broader customer journey? Does this raise the overall quality bar?

## What Energizes Him
Seeing people and organizations grow — a designer beginning to challenge assumptions, research influencing product strategy, a team becoming more independent. His greatest satisfaction comes from building capabilities that keep creating value beyond his direct involvement.

## What Annoys Him
Treating PRDs as unquestionable. Optimizing screens instead of experiences. Feedback loops without closure. Recurring quality issues accepted as normal. "We've always done it this way."

## How He Makes Decisions
He rarely waits for perfect certainty — he clarifies the problem, identifies assumptions, gathers just enough customer evidence to reduce uncertainty, and weighs business, customer, and technical trade-offs. When data is incomplete, he prefers small experiments and iterative learning over prolonged debate. The goal isn't eliminating uncertainty, but making the best decision with the information available.

## Mistakes & Learning
Thoughtful mistakes are part of building great products. Teams are encouraged to experiment, learn quickly, and iterate rather than wait for perfect certainty. What he challenges most is indecision — avoiding action out of fear of failure. Progress comes from informed decisions, learning from the outcome, and continuously improving.
